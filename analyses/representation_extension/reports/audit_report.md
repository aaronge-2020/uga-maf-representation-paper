## 3. Splits: the central structural finding

The archived main panel did not use one shared outer split. Each (endpoint, representation, learner) run drew its own partition. Verified directly: between the MAF_stack_only and signatures_plus_MAF_stack XGBoost runs, 7,051 of 8,800 cancer patients and 635 of 772 HRD patients sit in different folds, and same-label fold overlap is at chance level (~20 percent). Each run's partition is internally valid (one test fold per patient, no patient in two folds of the same run, confirmed this morning), so fold-mean estimators per run remain unbiased, and pooled patient-level pairing across runs stays valid because every patient has exactly one out-of-fold prediction per run. What the structure does not support is fold-paired comparisons across representations (paired t-tests on matched fold scores), because the folds contain different patients.

The repo's own convention for a canonical split, used by every MuAt config with `require_canonical_split: true`, is the OOF file filtered to representation `signatures_plus_MAF_stack`, learner `xgboost`, per endpoint. The new benchmark adopts exactly that as its single canonical split for both primary tasks (protocol amendment v1.1), so every new run is fold-aligned with the archived Sig+MAF reference run. Split files and hashes: `config/canonical_split_cancer.csv` (sha256 29d79f8f...), `config/canonical_split_hrd.csv` (sha256 c4175794...), provenance in `config/split_provenance.json`.

MuAt split manifests list patient barcodes per fold and role (`results/tables/muat_style_tcga_comparator_<tag>_split_manifest.csv`), but at least one arm's manifest disagrees with the main panel folds (task3 notes: 597 of 772 and 626 of 772 mismatched test assignments for arm C), so manifests are not used as the split source.

## 4. Existing comparisons: what can be reused, what cannot

Reusable as archived evidence (with estimator labels):
- Main panel endpoint and fold tables (96 endpoint rows, 480 fold rows) for the four manuscript representations across 13 endpoints, and the OOF predictions file, with one material caveat: for a subset of (endpoint, representation, learner) groups the saved OOF file does not reproduce the endpoint and fold tables (largest gaps listed in the same-day verification report, section "Inconsistencies inside the repo", item 13). The fold and endpoint tables agree with each other; the OOF file is the odd artifact for those groups. The new benchmark therefore treats archived fold tables as the reference for R6 point values and uses its own refits for primary contrasts.
- Task1 gene knowledge ablations (cancer typing, XGBoost): arms (a) full, (b) no knowledge columns, (c) no gene identity, (d) neither, (e) binary genes, (f) driver gene counts, for Bio MAF alone and Sig+MAF, with per-arm OOF files. Pooled balanced accuracies: Sig+MAF full 0.738, binary control 0.654, driver-count control 0.614; Bio MAF alone 0.662, binary 0.456, driver counts 0.307. Limit, verified in the producing script (`scripts/task1_gene_knowledge_ablations_v2.py`, header lines 15 to 17 and the `fit_fold` call path): every control refit reuses the full run's per-fold winner hyperparameters, `n_estimators` (m_star), feature-set search outcome, and seed schedule, with no re-tuning. The binary control is also narrower than its name: it is the indicator that the per-gene functional event log count exceeds 0, derived from the Bio MAF matrix, not an independent count from the MAF.
- HRD attribution baselines (task3): SBS3 alone, BRCA1/2 alone, both, with OOF files.
- HRD shortcut ablations, centre-held-out analysis, survival baselines, PCAWG and POG570 frozen-transfer outputs: reusable as archived results; the new Priority C work refits frozen models under the unified protocol rather than reusing these point values for the new contrasts.

Not existing anywhere in the repo (searched): any independently tuned comparison of a simple gene encoding against Bio MAF; any TF-IDF representation, script, or result (zero hits for tfidf in the whole tree); any CADD-weighted representation. These are the gaps Priority A fills.

- SBS3: `data/task3/sbs3_activity_772.csv` has exactly 9 of 772 patients with SBS3 greater than 0 (SigProfilerAssignment 1.1.5, COSMIC v3.4, GRCh37, fitted on the integer SBS96 counts in `data/task3/sbs96_counts_772.csv`; mean activity 1.54, max 250, per task3 notes). - BRCA: the task3 indicator (role-matched event log count greater than 0 for BRCA1 or BRCA2 in the Bio MAF matrix, patients truncated to 12 characters) is positive for exactly 14 of the 772 (204 of all 10,224). The manuscript's "fourteen" is correct.
- Interpretation limit, per the brief: these counts describe two narrow derived features (one fitted signature, one somatic role-matched mutation indicator). They carry no information about germline status, biallelic loss, copy number, or methylation, none of which is in the local data. Any wording that treats the 14 as "BRCA-deficient cases" or the 9 as "SBS3-positive tumours" in a biological sense is unsupported.

## 7. Centre aliquot duplicates (verified)

`results/tables/batch_center_sample_mapping.csv` has 10,226 tumour rows over 10,224 patients. TCGA-DV-A4W0 (KIRC) and TCGA-UZ-A9PS (KIRP) each contribute two aliquot rows (01A and 05A, both centre 10). In `task4_centre_heldout_predictions.csv` each patient therefore appears 4 times (2 aliquots x 2 representations) with identical predictions per aliquot. Effect on the reported centre-held-out recalls: the two patients are double-weighted in the KIRC and KIRP centre-10 counts (163 and 168 in the manuscript are barcode counts). The pooled recalls change by at most one patient weight in each type; the audit treats this as a documented weighting artefact, not leakage (both aliquots sit on the same held-out side). Any new centre analysis must aggregate to one row per patient first.

## 9. Prior exposure to test results (required disclosure)

This is a follow-up analysis of an existing study. The TCGA outer folds were used to produce the submitted manuscript's Table 1; PCAWG and POG570 results were examined, written up, and in part acted on (the manuscript reports a PRAD rescaling observation for PCAWG that has no source file in the repo). Every claim in the final package must be worded with that exposure in view.

## 10. Resource estimate and execution order

Measured on this machine: one untuned 60-tree XGBoost fit on a cancer fold (7,040 x 583) takes about 15 s; the tuned cell cost is dominated by 10 inner trials plus the final fit per fold. Estimated per-cell wall times: cancer XGBoost 20 to 60 min, cancer elastic net 10 to 40 min (saga solver), HRD cells 1 to 6 min. The Priority A grid (46 cells) is therefore a multi-hour sequential run on 2 cores; it runs in the fixed priority order of the protocol, primary combined panel cells first, so partial progress always covers the primary contrasts first. Priority B reuses the Bio MAF matrix with block subsets (same runner, block representations) after A. Priority C trains final models on the full cohorts and scores the saved external matrices. Priority D is table work plus matched-subset refits of the leading HRD cells. MuAt reruns and CADD weighting are blocked (hardware; missing licensed score source) and recorded as such.
