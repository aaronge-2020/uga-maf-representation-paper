## Results Section Text

We first established the strength of conventional mutational spectra relative to a minimal burden baseline. In the canonical main panel, XGBoost models using Mutational signatures outperformed Mutational burden across the main endpoint families: Kucab damage class improved from 0.505 to 0.636 macro-AUROC, HRD score from 0.596 to 0.704 Spearman correlation, HRD33 high/low from 0.811 to 0.845 AUROC, Cancer type (top 20) from 0.808 to 0.935 macro-AUROC, with survival endpoints reported by Cox PH C-index rather than binary OS AUROC. These gains were statistically significant for 6 of 6 tested XGBoost comparisons after FDR correction. Elastic net models showed the same qualitative gain for Kucab and cancer-type prediction, but not for every clinical or HRD endpoint; in particular, Mutational burden exceeded Mutational signatures for Elastic net HRD33 high/low. Thus, signatures are a strong baseline, but their advantage depends on both endpoint and model class.

We also added a direct neural event-bag comparator. The MuAt-compatible reimplementation uses mutation motif, 1-Mb position-bin, and annotation tokens with a Q/K/V attention architecture, and is evaluated on the manuscript's fixed 20-class TCGA-WES cancer-type endpoint. On the exact same Cancer type (top 20) outer folds, it reached 0.920 macro-AUROC, below the Signatures + Bio MAF v4 XGBoost tabular benchmark (0.976). This result should be interpreted as a faithful, comparable local reimplementation rather than as a claim about the official pretrained MuAt model.

Event-level MAF-stack features provided a complementary source of biological information. Bio MAF v4 alone was particularly useful for cancer-type prediction with XGBoost, improving over Mutational signatures from 0.935 to 0.959 macro-AUROC. However, it was not uniformly better than spectra: for Kucab damage class, Bio MAF v4 alone was lower than Mutational signatures with XGBoost (0.529 vs 0.636), consistent with the idea that mechanistic mutagen exposure is better captured by sequence-context or spectral information than by event-level gene/locus aggregates alone.

The strongest overall pattern emerged from combining spectra with event-level MAF features. Signatures + Bio MAF v4 was the best overall representation for most main endpoint families with XGBoost: HRD score reached 0.781 Spearman correlation, HRD33 high/low reached 0.867 AUROC, Cancer type (top 20) reached 0.976 macro-AUROC, and Cox PH Overall survival reached 0.633 C-index. The only main endpoint where it did not win was Kucab damage class, where Mutational signatures remained slightly higher for XGBoost. In pairwise tests, Signatures + Bio MAF v4 significantly improved over Bio MAF v4 alone in 5 of 10 Figure 4 comparisons and significantly improved over Mutational signatures alone for XGBoost HRD score, HRD42 high/low, Cancer type (top 20).

Taken together, the cross-endpoint summary shows that there is no single magic representation. Mutational signatures remain a strong and efficient baseline, Bio MAF v4 features capture endpoint-relevant biology that spectra alone can miss, and the MuAt-compatible event-bag comparator does not automatically exceed a tuned tabular baseline under this directly comparable evaluation. Across the main panel, the most robust practical default for tabular models is Signatures + Bio MAF v4, particularly when paired with XGBoost. The supplementary analyses document additional geometry variants, exposure checks, endpoint extensions, and completeness metadata without introducing N/A placeholders into the main manuscript figures.

## Statistical Notes

Primary metrics are pooled Spearman correlation for HRD score, AUROC for binary endpoints, macro-AUROC for multiclass endpoints, and Harrell C-index for Cox survival endpoints. Statistical statements refer to canonical/main_panel_pairwise_tests.csv, using paired DeLong tests for binary AUROC and paired bootstrap tests for macro-AUROC, C-index, or Spearman correlation.
